# Product design references — content organisation and detail pages

Research notes for the Volexar Studio application (dark, cinematic, premium AI filmmaking studio with
five primary areas: Shows, Shorts, Music Videos, Characters, Studio Company).

**Scope of these observations.** Everything below is *structural*: information architecture, hierarchy
of page sections, how detail pages are split into tabs/rails, and interaction patterns (hover, pickers,
tabs, rails, status). No branding, artwork, colour palettes, logos, iconography or exact layouts from any
of these products are to be copied. The third column of each table is our own translation into the
studio's vocabulary, and it is deliberately different in shape and purpose from the product observed.

Sources were read on 2026-10-02; only pages actually fetched are cited (see "Sources read").

---

## 1. Netflix — title detail overlay

Read: Pratt IxD design critique [1], BGR first look at the 2020 web redesign [2].

| Pattern observed | Why it works | What we translate into the studio (original) |
|---|---|---|
| Title detail is a single scrollable overlay, not separate pages: play/rate/add-to-list at top, then episodes grouped by season, then similar titles in a tile grid, then trailers/extras, then a flat "about" block with cast, genres, maturity rating [2]. | One scroll answers "what is it, can I start, what else is there" without leaving the browse context; the old tab system forced round trips. | Show pages open as a full page (we have production controls), but the *order* is kept: identity and primary action first, then structure (seasons/episodes), then people, then details. Production controls sit in a separate rail, never between content sections. |
| Hover on a browse tile reveals a lightweight tooltip (match %, genres) before a preview starts; feedback arrives in well under a second [1]. | Instant feedback while heavier media loads (Doherty threshold); decisions can be made from metadata alone. | Catalogue cards show a status/metadata strip on hover (season count, last render, pipeline status) before any preview; preview is optional and deferred. |
| Season 1 is shown first on the detail page even when the newest season is the reason the user arrived [1]. | Noted as a *weakness*: the picker defaults to the oldest content. | Season picker defaults to the most recently *touched* season (in production or last published); explicit dropdown/segmented control to switch. |
| Uniform tile rows make originals, new releases and catalogue indistinguishable [1]. | Lack of hierarchy was flagged as a usability gap. | Our library differentiates by *shape and state*, not by badges alone: wide key art for shows, poster for shorts, square sleeve for music, portrait for characters (see section 9.7). |
| A like/rate control whose popover overlapped neighbouring buttons caused mis-clicks [1]. | Error prevention: secondary menus should not cover primary actions. | Production action menus (render, regenerate, approve) open *beside* the control, never over the primary "Play" or "Produce" button. |

## 2. Disney+ — brand rows and title tabs

Read: Disney+ Extras walkthrough on PS5 [3]. (Search snippets from Tom's Guide and
whatsondisneyplus confirmed the same tab names but those pages were not readable; not cited.)

| Pattern observed | Why it works | What we translate into the studio (original) |
|---|---|---|
| Below the hero/play button a title page is split into named tabs: Suggested (default), Extras, plus Episodes and Details for series [3]. | The hero stays fixed while the lower half swaps content; one page, several "lenses". Extras keeps bonus material (deleted scenes, interviews, promos, trailers) out of the main episode list [3]. | Show page uses a sticky identity header plus a tab strip: Overview · Seasons · Episodes · Cast · Production. "Extras" becomes **Deliverables** (trailers, stills, promo cuts generated by the studio) in the Production tab. |
| Brand tiles/hubs (studio brands) as a top-level navigation row above genre rows (search snippet, [3] context). | Brand is a first-class filter; users think "Pixar", not "animated comedy". | Our equivalent of brand hubs is the **Studio Company**: departments are the "brands" that produce the content, and each has a hub page (section 9.6). |
| Extras is explicitly a *separate* tab reached by moving right from Suggested on a TV remote [3]. | Lean-back navigation: horizontal movement between tabs, vertical movement within. | Keyboard navigation in the studio mirrors this: ←/→ switches tabs, ↑/↓ moves within a list; works for a desk-bound producer and for a 10-foot review room. |

## 3. Apple TV (tv.apple.com / TV app) — show page

Read: Apple Support "TV Shows in the Apple TV app" [4].

| Pattern observed | Why it works | What we translate into the studio (original) |
|---|---|---|
| Show page order: key art and summary → "How to Watch" row → Episodes (pick a season, then browse episodes) → Cast & Crew row (tap a person for bio and credits) → Related row → ratings/description/other information at the bottom [4]. | Sections are horizontal rails under a vertical scroll; the most actionable row ("how to watch") is first; details are last because they are reference, not decision. | Show page: key art and eyebrow metadata → **Produce / Watch** row (latest cut, next episode to generate) → Episodes rail with season selector → Cast rail (tap → Character profile) → Related shows → Details block. |
| Season selection precedes episode browsing: choose a season, then the episode list reloads [4]. | Two-level picker keeps a 10-season show manageable. | Seasons tab = grid of season cards (status, episode count, arc summary). Episodes tab = list filtered by a season control; same data, two densities. |
| Cast & Crew is a rail of people with their own pages listing "associated films and TV programmes" [4]. | People are entities that cross titles. | Characters are first-class entities (casting directory). A Character profile lists every show/short/music video it appears in, with role per appearance. |

## 4. IMDb — title page sections and credits

Read: IMDb Help "Title Full Credits redesign" [5]; a community page-cleaner script that enumerates the title page sections it reorders [6].

| Pattern observed | Why it works | What we translate into the studio (original) |
|---|---|---|
| Title page is a long, sectioned reference page: header with ratings → Videos/Photos → Top cast → Storyline → User reviews → Details → Did you know → Box office → Technical specs → More like this [6]. | Everything about a title in one URL; sections are jump targets. Community scripts exist purely to *reorder* and *remove* sections, which shows the sections are well-defined units [6]. | Each detail page is built from named, reorderable section components (Overview, Cast, Storyline, Details, Technical). Users can collapse sections; the Production tab can show the same section ids in "editing" mode. |
| Full credits page: credits grouped by category with a "Jump to" menu to reach a category directly; responsive across web and mobile [5]. | Long structured lists need a table of contents, not pagination. | Studio Company "Credits" view per title: grouped by department (Story, Casting, Art, Production, Sound, Editorial, QC) with a jump menu. Cast page uses the same grouping (lead / supporting / featured). |
| "Top cast" is a short rail; full cast is one click away [5][6]. | Progressive disclosure: hero cast on the page, exhaustive list on demand. | Show Overview shows up to ~8 cast portraits; Cast tab holds everyone plus casting controls (recast, voice, wardrobe). |

## 5. Spotify — artist/album header and lyrics

Read: RouteNote on the single-image artist header [7]; Hypebot on the global Lyrics launch [8].

| Pattern observed | Why it works | What we translate into the studio (original) |
|---|---|---|
| Artist page moved from two images (avatar + banner) to a single full-bleed header image; the artist "defines themselves with one image" [7]. | One hero image = one identity; reduces art-management burden and gives the page a cinematic opening. | Music Video page opens with one square sleeve as the identity object and a derived, blurred wash behind it; no separate avatar for the "artist" (the singing character is linked in the Cast row instead). |
| Lyrics live inside Now Playing: swipe up on mobile, microphone icon on desktop, toggle on TV; lines scroll in real time; selected lines can be shared as a card [8]. | Lyrics are a *mode of the player*, time-synced to playback, not a static text block. | Lyrics panel is a mode of the song player: time-synced lines, each line carries a **singer assignment** (which character sings it); clicking a line seeks the player; a selection can be exported as a lyric card. |
| Dense track list with a single prominent play control in the header (search snippets; not fetched, not cited). | — | Track/section list under the player: intro, verse, chorus, bridge rows with duration, assigned singer, shot status. |

## 6. Apple Music — album page, now playing, lyrics view

Read: Blake Crosley design guide to Apple Music [9].

| Pattern observed | Why it works | What we translate into the studio (original) |
|---|---|---|
| Album artwork fills the screen and *tints the UI*; the Now Playing backdrop is a gradient extracted from the artwork [9]. | Each album gets a unique visual environment with zero per-title design work. | Each Music Video and Show derives an accent from its own artwork (we compute it; we do not copy any palette). The base design system stays dark and neutral; only the accent moves. |
| Now Playing: full-width cover, metadata, scrubber, transport, then tabs Lyrics · Up Next · Related [9]. | Player first, context tabs second. | Music Video page: video player (16:9) and song player share one transport; tabs under it: Lyrics · Shots · Cast · Production · Export. |
| Lyrics view is full-screen: active line bright and scaled, past lines blur/dim, future lines reduced opacity, word-level highlight, tap a line to seek, heavily blurred art behind [9]. | The current line is the only thing that matters; everything else is context. | Lyrics with singer assignment use the same focus logic: active line at full weight with the singer's portrait chip; other lines dimmed; a per-line assignment control appears on hover/focus. |
| Editorial attribution ("By <curator>") and persistent but quiet quality labels (Lossless, Atmos) under track info [9]. | Human authorship and technical state are shown without shouting. | Every deliverable shows a quiet "Produced by <Department> · <model>" eyebrow and a quality badge from QC (pass / warnings / fail). |

## 7. YouTube Music — artist and album pages

Read: 9to5Google on the 2024 artist page redesign [10]; Android Police on the album page redesign [11].

| Pattern observed | Why it works | What we translate into the studio (original) |
|---|---|---|
| Artist page: name left-aligned beside subscriber count, pill "Subscribe", circular secondary actions (radio/shuffle), then **Latest release** card, then Top songs with a chevron to expand, then the rest of the feed [10]. | Left-aligned identity block + one primary pill + small circular secondary actions = compact, scannable header. "Latest release" before "Top songs" favours newness. | Character profile header: name, role count, one primary action ("Cast in…"), small circular actions (voice test, regenerate look). Then **Latest appearance** card, then "Appears in" rail, then details. |
| Album page: centred cover with blurred version behind; artist · type · year *above* the title, description below; one row of five actions (download, add, play, share, more); song count and total duration at the foot of the track list; floating play button while scrolling [11]. | Metadata eyebrow above the title reads as a label; one action row keeps everything within thumb reach; totals at the bottom close the list. | Music Video page header: eyebrow "Music Video · <year> · <duration>" above the title; one action row (Play, Produce, Export, Share, More); the sections list ends with total runtime and shot count; the transport stays sticky while scrolling lyrics. |
| Shuffle demoted to overflow when not primary [11]. | Reduce the action row to what matters per context. | Per-page action rows are capped at five; the rest goes to "More". |

## 8. Production and post software

Read: StudioBinder product page [12]; Envato Tuts+ Frame.io collaboration tutorial [13]; Firecut and TourBox articles on DaVinci Resolve pages [14][15]; ProVideo Coalition on Premiere Pro Productions vs Avid bins [16].

| Pattern observed | Why it works | What we translate into the studio (original) |
|---|---|---|
| StudioBinder organises a project by *stage modules*: Write (scripts) → Breakdown (tag elements, stripboard schedule) → Visualize (mood boards, shot lists, storyboards) → Plan (contacts, calendar, task boards, media library) → Shoot (call sheets, distribution, delivery tracking). Data flows forward: schedule into call sheets, cast into sides [12]. | The pipeline is the navigation; each module owns one document type; downstream documents are auto-populated. | Each Department page owns its document types (Story: outline/script; Casting: character sheets; Art: look boards; Production: shot lists/schedules; Sound: stems; Editorial: cuts; QC: reports). Objects created upstream appear as inputs downstream without re-entry. |
| Frame.io: projects → folders → assets; new uploads stack as versions you can toggle and compare; comments are frame-accurate and pen-annotated; a client can mark a version approved; comments round-trip into the NLE attached to clips [13]. | Review is organised around *versions* and *timecoded comments*, with an explicit approval state. | Every deliverable (episode cut, shot, song mix, lyric video) has a version stack, timecoded notes and a status (draft → in review → approved → published). QC results attach to a version, not to the title. |
| DaVinci Resolve: seven pages in a fixed left-to-right order — Media, Cut, Edit, Fusion, Color, Fairlight, Deliver — each a dedicated workspace; move right as the project matures [14][15]. | A page per discipline keeps each tool-set focused; the order encodes the workflow. | The Studio Company's department list is ordered as a pipeline (Story → Casting → Art Direction → Production → Sound → Editorial → QC → Delivery) and the Production tab on any title shows the same order as a stage strip with per-stage status. |
| Premiere Productions: a production folder holds many small projects (by reel/scene, or by season for episodic), 1:1 with disk folders, shared media, project-level locking while someone edits; a project behaves like an Avid bin [16]. | Decompose a large show into lockable units; episodic work groups by season. | Shows decompose into Season → Episode → Scene/Shot units; an agent "checks out" a unit while working (visible lock + who/why), others can read and copy but not write. |

## 9. How real studios organise departments

Read: Pixar production process summary [17]; Studio Liddell animation pipeline guide [18]; Pixune VFX pipeline breakdown [19].

| Pattern observed | Why it works | What we translate into the studio (original) |
|---|---|---|
| Pixar sequence: pitch → text treatment → storyboards → scratch/voice recording → editorial reels → art department look → modelling/rigging → set dressing → layout (camera/staging) → animation → shading → lighting → rendering → music and sound [17]. | Story and editorial start *before* assets exist; reels validate the story cheaply; layout gives editorial choices before expensive animation. | Story department produces outline → script → boards → animatic for every episode before any render is scheduled; Editorial owns the reel from day one; Art Direction locks the look before Production generates shots. |
| Animation pipeline phases: pre-production (script, visual development, boards, voice, animatic) → production (modelling, texturing, rigging, layout, animation, FX, lighting/render) → post (compositing, fixes, edit refinement, sound design and mix, colour, **QC and delivery verification** — approved masters, named version sets, archive-safe organisation) [18]. | QC is an explicit department with its own acceptance criteria: "visually complete" is not "deliverable". | QC is a department with a page, a checklist per deliverable type, and a results feed; Delivery is gated on QC pass. Export produces named version sets with manifests. |
| VFX departments split into *asset* departments (modelling, texturing, look-dev, rigging) and *shot* departments (layout, animation, FX, lighting, rendering, compositing) [19]. | Reusable things (characters, sets) are built once; shots consume them. | Characters, locations and props are **assets** owned by Casting/Art; episodes and shots are **work** owned by Production/Editorial. The Character profile therefore shows "used in N shots" and never duplicates per episode. |

## 10. Information architecture for this product

### 10.1 Primary areas and what is secondary
Primary navigation (always visible, in this order): **Shows · Shorts · Music Videos · Characters · Studio**.
Secondary (reachable from a global "Activity" button, from any Production tab, or from a settings menu):
production activity feed, job queue, logs, QC reports, model/tool configuration, exports/archive, settings.
Secondary surfaces never appear in the primary bar; they slide in as a right-hand drawer so the title
being worked on stays in view.

### 10.2 Show detail page
- Header: wide key art (16:9 or wider), eyebrow metadata (Show · seasons · episodes · status · runtime),
  title, logline, primary actions (Play latest / Produce next), one row of secondary actions.
- Tabs: **Overview** (synopsis, latest episode card, top cast rail, related shows, details block) ·
  **Seasons** (season cards: arc summary, episode count, stage strip, status) ·
  **Episodes** (season selector + list: number, title, duration, cut version, QC badge, stage) ·
  **Cast** (grouped lead/supporting/featured, each → Character profile; recast/voice controls) ·
  **Production** (pipeline stage strip, assigned departments/agents, version stacks, timecoded notes,
  checkout locks, deliverables/extras, activity).
- Episode page inherits the show header; adds script/boards/animatic/cut tabs and a shot list.

### 10.3 Short detail page
- Header: poster (2:3) left, metadata right (Short · genre · runtime · status), logline, Play / Produce.
- Sections: Overview (synopsis, cast rail, related shorts) · Script & Boards · Shots (shot list with
  version/status) · Cast · Production (same component as shows) · Deliverables (master, trailer, stills).

### 10.4 Music Video page
- Header: square sleeve (1:1) with blurred derived wash, eyebrow "Music Video · year · duration",
  title, artist/character, one action row (Play, Produce, Export, Share, More).
- Players: video player (16:9) and song player share one sticky transport.
- Tabs: **Lyrics** (time-synced lines, active line emphasised, singer assignment chip per line,
  click to seek, select lines → lyric card) · **Sections** (intro/verse/chorus rows with duration,
  singer, shot status, totals at foot) · **Cast** (performers, singers, dancers) · **Production**
  (stage strip, versions, notes, QC) · **Export** (masters, stems, lyric video, vertical cut, manifest).

### 10.5 Character profile
- Header: portrait (3:4), name, role summary, one primary action (Cast in…), small secondary actions.
- Sections: Latest appearance card · Appears in (shows/shorts/music videos with role) · Voice & look
  (reference sheets, voice samples, variants) · Relationships · Details (bio, traits, canonical facts) ·
  Production (owned by Casting; regenerate look, version stack of reference sheets, QC on consistency).

### 10.6 Studio Company and Department pages
- Studio page: company overview, pipeline strip (Story → Casting → Art Direction → Production →
  Sound → Editorial → QC → Delivery), department cards with director, active assignments count,
  queue depth, last QC result; global activity feed; models and tools inventory.
- Department page sections: **Director** (agent in charge) · **Agents** (roster with role) ·
  **Responsibilities** (document types owned, inputs/outputs) · **Models** (which generation/eval
  models, versions) · **Tools & skills** · **Active assignments** (title/unit, stage, lock holder, ETA) ·
  **Deliverables** (version stacks produced) · **Activity** (timecoded log) · **Quality results**
  (QC pass/warn/fail trend, open issues).
- Credits view per title groups contributions by department with a jump menu.

### 10.7 Visual differentiation within one design system
- One dark, neutral base; typography and spacing identical everywhere; one accent *derived per title*
  from its own artwork, never a fixed per-section brand colour.
- Artwork shape is the primary differentiator: Shows = wide key art; Shorts = poster 2:3;
  Music Videos = square sleeve 1:1; Characters = portrait 3:4; Studio/Departments = no artwork,
  an abstract stage-strip glyph instead.
- Typography: display face for titles on content pages; the Studio Company uses the same family at
  text sizes with tabular numerals for status, queues and QC figures.
- Eyebrow metadata line above every title (type · count · duration · status) is the one constant
  that tells the user which area they are in.
